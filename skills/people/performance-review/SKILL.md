# Performance Review

Write a review built on what someone actually did, not a halo or a recency bias, so the
feedback is fair and the person knows exactly what to keep doing and what to change.
This is guidance, not HR or legal advice.

## When to use

Use this skill when preparing a periodic performance review, a written feedback summary,
or self-review input, and you want it grounded in evidence rather than impressions. It
drafts the review; final ratings and process follow your own HR policy.

## How to prepare the review

1. **Collect evidence across the whole period first.** Pull specific examples from the
   full cycle — shipped work, outcomes, peer and stakeholder input — not just the last month.
   - Tie each example to a date and a result so it is concrete, not a vague vibe.
   - Cover the entire period deliberately to counter recency bias.
2. **Map evidence to expectations, not to personality.** Judge against the role's goals
   and level, and assess the work and behavior — never the person's character or traits.
   - Watch for bias: rate consistently, and avoid letting one strong or weak moment color
     everything.
3. **Structure feedback in three parts.** Strengths (what to keep doing), growth areas
   (what to improve), and goals (what to do next) — each anchored to a real example.
   - For every growth area, name the specific behavior and the better version, not a label.
   - Lead with evidence, then the takeaway: "On X you did Y, which led to Z."
4. **Make goals specific and ownable.** Each goal states the outcome, a rough measure, and
   a timeframe, so progress is checkable at the next cycle.
   - Two or three real goals beat ten aspirational ones.
5. **Keep the tone direct and respectful.** Be honest about gaps without softening them
   into mush; be specific in praise so it lands as earned, not as padding.

## Output format

```
Review: <name> · Period: <range>
Summary: 2–3 sentences — overall, evidence-based.
Strengths:
- <behavior> — e.g. <example/outcome>
Growth areas:
- <behavior> — e.g. <example>; better: <specific change>
Goals (next period):
- <outcome> — measure: <how> · by: <when>
```

If a point has no concrete example behind it, cut it or go find the evidence.
