# Interview Prep

Decide what you are measuring and how you will score it before the interview, so the
loop is consistent and comparable instead of a charisma contest. This is guidance, not
HR or legal advice.

## When to use

Use this skill when preparing to interview for a role, designing an interview loop, or
fixing inconsistent, gut-feel hiring decisions. It assumes the role and must-have
criteria already exist — `hiring` produces those.

## How to build the kit

1. **Pick four to six competencies, not more.** Derive them straight from the role's
   must-haves so the loop tests the job, not trivia.
   - Split them across interviewers so each owns two or three and no area is double-
     covered or skipped.
2. **Write a question bank per competency.** Mix behavioral ("tell me about a time…")
   with role-specific or practical prompts that surface real ability.
   - Behavioral questions probe past behavior; follow up for situation, action, and
     result until you have evidence, not a slogan.
   - Ask every candidate the same core questions so answers are comparable.
3. **Define a scorecard with explicit anchors.** One row per competency, a 1–4 scale,
   and a written description of what a 1 versus a 4 looks like.
   - Force a per-competency rating plus evidence; ban a single blurred overall vibe.
   - Use a hire / no-hire / strong call, decided after notes are in — not during.
4. **Keep it fair and bias-aware.** Same questions, same rubric, same time budget for
   everyone. Score against the anchors, not against the last candidate.
   - Avoid culture-"fit" as a criterion; assess values and collaboration concretely.
   - Have interviewers submit scores independently before debriefing together.
5. **Run a debrief on evidence.** Each interviewer reads their rating and the example
   behind it; the panel decides on the aggregate, not the loudest voice.

A reusable scorecard template lives in `references/scorecard.md` — copy it per role and
fill the anchors.

## Output format

```
Role: <title>
Competencies: 1) … 2) … 3) … 4) …
Per competency: 2–3 questions (behavioral + practical) · interviewer owner
Scorecard: competency | 1–4 anchors | rating | evidence
Decision: independent scores → debrief → hire / no-hire
```
