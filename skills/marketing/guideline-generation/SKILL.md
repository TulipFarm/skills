# Brand Guideline Generation

Brand guidelines are a tool for the people creating content — not a declaration
of brand identity for its own sake. Good guidelines are specific enough to resolve
real writing decisions and short enough to be read.

## When to use

Use this skill after completing a brand discovery (see discover-brand), or when
a brand has been defined strategically and needs to be translated into a practical
writing guide. Input is source material: brand strategy documents, existing
content samples, discovery findings, and any visual identity assets.

## How to generate brand guidelines

1. **Start with brand purpose and positioning.** One to two sentences:
   - Who the brand serves.
   - What the brand does.
   - What makes it distinct.
   This is not a tagline — it is the north star content writers use when in doubt.

2. **Define the voice.** Voice is the consistent personality — it does not change
   by channel. Choose three to five attributes. For each:
   - Name the attribute.
   - Explain what it means for this brand specifically (not generically).
   - Show a do/don't example side by side.
   Generic attributes like "friendly" or "professional" mean nothing without
   examples that show exactly what they mean here.

3. **Define the tone spectrum.** Tone shifts by context; voice does not.
   Document the appropriate tone for each major content type:
   - Acquisition marketing (ads, social, landing pages)
   - Product and in-app copy
   - Onboarding and education
   - Customer support and error messages
   - Corporate and press communications
   For each, state the tone adjustment (e.g., "More formal — never use contractions
   in press releases").

4. **Write vocabulary rules.** Three lists:
   - **Use:** specific words, phrases, and naming conventions the brand prefers.
   - **Avoid:** words that are off-brand, clichéd, or create the wrong impression.
   - **Product and feature names:** exact official names and how to refer to them.
   Rules must be specific. "Be clear" is not a rule. "Do not use the word 'leverage'
   as a verb" is a rule.

5. **Document grammar and style preferences.** Sentence length, capitalization
   rules (product names, job titles), Oxford comma usage, number formatting,
   and any deviations from the style guide standard.

6. **Provide channel adaptation guidance.** For each key channel (website, email,
   social, in-product), state: word count norms, tone adjustments, and any
   format-specific rules.

7. **Include a writing checklist.** A short list of questions a writer can ask
   before publishing any piece: "Does this sound like us?" "Is the first sentence
   pulling the reader in?" "Is there a clear next step?"

## Output format

```
Brand Guidelines: <company name>
Version: 1.0 | Date: <date>

---

## Brand in one paragraph
<Purpose, audience, and distinction — the reference point for every content decision>

## Voice

### <Attribute 1>
What this means for us: <specific explanation>
✓ Do: "<example>"
✗ Don't: "<example>"

### <Attribute 2–5>
[repeat]

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| Acquisition marketing | <e.g., Confident, direct> | <specific rule> |
| In-product / UX copy | ... | ... |
| Support | ... | ... |
| Press / corporate | ... | ... |

## Vocabulary

### Use these
- <word or phrase> — <why and when>

### Avoid these
- <word> — <why>

### Product and feature names
- <Feature>: always written as <exact name>; never shortened to <X>

## Style preferences
- Sentence length: <rule>
- Capitalization: <rule>
- Numbers: <rule>
- Oxford comma: <yes/no>

## Channel guidance

### <Channel name>
- Length: <norm>
- Format: <structure notes>
- Tone adjustment: <what changes>

## Writer checklist
- [ ] Does this sound like us?
- [ ] Is the first sentence earning its place?
- [ ] Is there one clear call to action?
- [ ] <Brand-specific check>
```
