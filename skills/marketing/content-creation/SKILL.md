# Content Creation

Write long-form content that earns the reader's attention and moves them toward one
action — not filler that fills a content calendar.

## When to use

Use this skill when asked to draft a blog post, newsletter, guide, or landing-page
section from a brief or topic. For short persuasive copy — headlines, CTAs, subject
lines, ad hooks — use `copywriting` instead.

## How to draft

1. **Pin down the brief before writing a word.** Confirm four things: who the reader
   is, what they should feel or do by the end, the single core message, and the format.
   If any is missing, state your assumption or ask — do not guess silently.
2. **Lead with the reader's problem, not your product.** Open on the tension or
   question they already have. Earn the rest of the piece in the first two sentences.
3. **Outline to the goal, then write.** One idea per section, ordered so each answers
   the question the last one raised. Cut any section that does not serve the core
   message.
4. **Write in the brand voice.** Concrete over abstract, specific over generic, active
   over passive. Short sentences carry the weight; long ones explain. If a brand-voice
   guide exists, follow it; otherwise default to plain, confident, jargon-free.
5. **Make claims earn their place.** Support assertions with a reason, example, or
   number. Replace adjectives ("powerful", "seamless") with evidence the reader can
   check.
6. **Close with one next step.** Every piece ends pointing somewhere — subscribe, read
   the next guide, start a trial, reply. One CTA, not a menu.

## Output format

Return a ready-to-edit draft:

```
Title / subject: <option>
Audience: <who> · Goal: <desired action>
---
<the draft, with headings and short paragraphs>
---
CTA: <single next step>
Notes: <open questions, places needing a fact, suggested visual>
```

Mark anything you assumed or invented so an editor can verify it. A draft with a made-up
statistic is worse than a draft with a flagged gap.
