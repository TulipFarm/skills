# Copywriting Framework Cheatsheet

One page of proven structures. Pick one per asset, fill the beats, then cut.

## AIDA — ads, landing pages, pitches

Move the reader from cold to action in four beats.

- **Attention** — a hook that interrupts: a bold claim, a sharp question, a surprising
  number.
- **Interest** — make it relevant: name the reader's situation or desire.
- **Desire** — make them want it: paint the outcome, add proof (a number, a result).
- **Action** — tell them exactly what to do next, once and clearly.

Example beat: "Still chasing invoices? → Most small teams lose 6 hours a week to it. →
Get paid in two days, automatically. → Start free."

## PAS — pain-driven hooks, cold outreach, social

Best when the reader feels a problem more than a desire.

- **Problem** — name the pain in their words.
- **Agitate** — show the cost of leaving it unsolved; make it vivid, not preachy.
- **Solve** — present your offer as the relief, with the single next step.

Example: "Late invoices pile up. → Every week unpaid is cash you can't spend on growth.
→ Automate reminders and get paid on time."

## The 4 U's — pressure-test any headline or subject line

Score the line on each; a strong line hits at least three.

- **Useful** — does it promise a clear benefit?
- **Urgent** — is there a reason to act now?
- **Unique** — does it say something only you can?
- **Ultra-specific** — concrete numbers, names, timeframes — not vague claims?

If a headline scores low, the fastest fixes are adding a number (specific) or a
timeframe (urgent).

## FAB — feature-heavy products

Translate what you built into what they get.

- **Feature** — what it is.
- **Advantage** — what it does.
- **Benefit** — what the reader gains. Lead the copy with the benefit; mention the
  feature last, if at all.

## Quick rules across all frameworks

- One idea per line. One action per asset.
- Benefit before feature, specific before vague, active before passive.
- Cut every word that the sentence survives without.
- Read it aloud; if you stumble, rewrite it.
- Match the brand voice — a framework structures the message, it does not set the tone.
