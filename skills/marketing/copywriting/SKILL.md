# Copywriting

Write short copy that gets clicked, opened, or acted on. Every word fights for the next
one to be read.

## When to use

Use this skill when asked for headlines, calls to action, email subject lines, ad copy,
social hooks, taglines, or any short persuasive line. For blog posts, newsletters, or
page-length content, use `content-creation`.

## How to write

1. **Get the one thing straight.** Confirm the single most valuable outcome for the
   reader and the one action you want. Copy that sells two things sells neither.
2. **Pick a framework on purpose.** Reach for a proven structure rather than freestyling
   — **AIDA** (attention → interest → desire → action) for ads and pages, **PAS**
   (problem → agitate → solve) for pain-driven hooks, **the 4 U's** (useful, urgent,
   unique, ultra-specific) to pressure-test a headline. See
   `references/frameworks.md` for the cheatsheet.
3. **Lead with benefit, not feature.** "Get paid in two days" beats "Automated payment
   processing". Translate every feature into what the reader gets.
4. **Be specific and concrete.** Numbers, timeframes, and named outcomes beat vague
   superlatives. Cut "best", "amazing", "revolutionary" — they signal nothing.
5. **Write several distinct angles, not minor rewordings.** Vary the emotional appeal:
   one on gain, one on loss/fear-of-missing, one on curiosity, one plain and direct.
6. **Read it aloud and cut.** If a word can go, it goes. Match the brand voice; if a
   voice guide exists, conform to it.

## Output format

```
Asset: <headline | CTA | subject line | hook> · Goal: <action>
1. "<option>" — framework: <AIDA/PAS/4U>, angle: <gain/fear/curiosity/direct>
2. "<option>" — framework: <…>, angle: <…>
3. "<option>" — framework: <…>, angle: <…>
Recommendation: <which to lead with and why>
```

Give at least three labeled options. Never imply test results you do not have — frame
strong claims as "worth A/B testing", not "proven to convert".
