# Create Marketing Asset

An asset without a clear goal and audience is content for content's sake. Every
asset this skill produces starts with the strategy — who it is for, what it must
make them think or do — before a single word of copy is written.

## When to use

Use this skill when you need a specific piece of marketing content: a paid ad,
email campaign, landing page, one-pager leave-behind, or social post. The input
is the asset type, the campaign goal, the target audience, and any brand or
messaging constraints. Do not use it to produce bulk generic content — the value
comes from specificity.

## How to create a marketing asset

1. **Define the brief before writing.** For any asset, answer:
   - **Asset type:** ad copy / landing page / one-pager / social post / email / other.
   - **Campaign goal:** the single action the audience should take (sign up,
     download, request demo, share, reply).
   - **Audience:** who exactly is seeing this — their role, awareness level, pain point.
   - **Primary message:** the one thing they must remember or believe after seeing it.
   - **Proof points:** facts, numbers, or testimonials that support the primary message.
   - **Brand voice:** tone attributes or reference to brand guidelines.
   - **Constraints:** word count limits, legal / compliance restrictions, channel rules.
   Do not start writing without this brief. If the brief is incomplete, ask.

2. **Lead with the audience, not the product.** The first sentence of every asset
   must address something the audience cares about — their pain, their goal, their
   situation — not the company or product. If the first sentence could be said
   about any product for any audience, rewrite it.

3. **Write to the format.** Different assets have different structural requirements:
   - **Ad copy:** headline (3–7 words), body (1–2 sentences max), CTA.
   - **Landing page:** above-the-fold headline + subhead, benefits section,
     proof section (social proof / stats), single CTA, optional FAQ.
   - **One-pager:** problem / solution / proof / next step structure; fits one page.
   - **Email:** subject line + preview text, hook in first sentence, body,
     single CTA — under 200 words for marketing emails.
   - **Social post:** platform character limit, hook in first line, CTA or
     engagement question at end.

4. **Apply one message per asset.** Every element of the asset should serve the
   primary message. Remove anything that dilutes or distracts. An asset trying
   to say three things says nothing.

5. **Write the CTA specifically.** "Learn more" is not a CTA. "See how X company
   cut their onboarding time in half" is. The CTA must tell the reader what
   they get, not just what to click.

6. **Produce multiple headline options.** For any asset with a headline (which is
   most), write at least three options using different approaches: benefit-led,
   curiosity-led, and outcome-led. Let the team choose — do not pick one and hide
   the others.

## Output format

```
Asset: <type>
Campaign: <goal> | Audience: <persona> | Date: <date>

## Brief summary
- Primary message: <one sentence>
- Proof point(s): <key supporting facts>
- Tone: <voice attributes>
- Constraints: <word count, legal, etc.>

---

## Draft

### Headline options
1. <Benefit-led>
2. <Curiosity-led>
3. <Outcome-led>

### Body copy
<Full draft — formatted for the asset type>

### CTA
<Specific call to action text>

---

## Copy notes
- Why the lead works for this audience: <rationale>
- Proof points used and source: <list>
- What was cut and why: <any deliberate omissions>

## Variants (if requested)
- A/B variant: <alternate headline or body>
```
