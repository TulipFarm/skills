# Legal Response

A legal response must be precise, professional, and complete. It must not
concede points inadvertently, fail to address a claim, or make admissions
that create liability. Always have legal counsel review before sending any
response to a legal notice.

**Important:** This skill produces a draft for attorney review. Do not send
any response to a legal notice without review by qualified legal counsel.

## When to use

Use this skill to create a structured first draft when you receive a cease-and-desist
letter, a demand letter, a regulatory inquiry, or a formal legal notice. The draft
is for your attorney to review and finalize. It is also useful for organizing your
facts and position before the attorney call, so you make efficient use of billable
time.

## How to draft a legal response

1. **Identify every claim and demand.** Read the incoming notice carefully and
   list every distinct claim, allegation, and demand made. Number them. Missing
   a claim in the response is worse than addressing it — unanswered claims can
   be construed as admissions.

2. **Gather the relevant facts.** For each claim, document the facts you have
   available that are relevant. Separate facts you know to be true, facts you
   believe to be true but cannot confirm, and facts you dispute. Do not state
   uncertain facts as certain in the response.

3. **Determine your position on each claim.** For each claim:
   - **Deny:** you dispute the factual or legal basis.
   - **Acknowledge:** you accept the factual assertion but dispute its legal
     characterization or remedy.
   - **Explain:** the claim is based on a misunderstanding you can clarify.
   Avoid "no comment" — every claim gets a stated position.

4. **Preserve all rights.** The response must include language preserving your
   rights and defenses — this is standard and must not be omitted. Include a
   statement that the response is not a waiver of any rights, remedies, or defenses.

5. **Propose a resolution path.** Where appropriate, propose a concrete next step:
   a meeting to discuss, a timeline for further information, a proposed resolution.
   A response that only says "we disagree" rarely ends the matter.

6. **Keep the tone professional and neutral.** Legal responses are often read by
   judges, regulators, and mediators. Emotional language, accusations, or threats
   undermine credibility. State your position firmly but professionally.

7. **Format as a formal letter.** Date, reference to the original notice, formal
   salutation, numbered response to each claim, closing with preserved rights
   language, and signature block.

## Output format

```
[DRAFT — FOR ATTORNEY REVIEW BEFORE SENDING]

<Date>

<Sender name and address>

Re: Response to <Notice type> dated <date> — <Reference number if any>

Dear <Name / Counsel>,

We write in response to your letter dated <date> ("the Notice") received by
<company name> on <date>. We address each claim in turn.

---

**Preliminary statement**
<Any factual corrections about the parties, jurisdiction, or context that should
be established before addressing claims.>

---

**Response to Claim 1: <Claim title from notice>**

<Your position — deny / acknowledge / explain — with factual basis stated
factually, not emotionally.>

**Response to Claim 2: ...**

[Repeat for each claim]

---

**Reservation of rights**

Nothing in this letter constitutes a waiver of any rights, remedies, defenses,
or claims available to <company name>, all of which are expressly reserved.

**Proposed next steps**

<Proposed resolution path or next communication.>

Sincerely,

<Signature block>

---

[DRAFT NOTES FOR ATTORNEY]
- Claim <n>: uncertain fact — confirm before finalizing
- Need attorney to review <specific section>
- Open legal questions: <list>
```
