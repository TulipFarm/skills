# Customer Feedback Analysis

Turn ongoing feedback into an operational view of what customers experience and what may
deserve action. Count patterns without flattening important differences between segments.

## When to use

Use this skill for recurring analysis of existing comments, requests, reviews, survey
responses, interview notes, and service conversations. Use customer-research when the task
is to design a study, recruit participants, or create a research instrument.

## How to analyze customer feedback

1. **Define the decision window.** State the period, feedback sources, customer segments,
   products or workflows, and decisions the analysis should inform.
2. **Assess source coverage.** Count available items by source and segment, note missing
   periods or channels, and identify collection bias before interpreting totals.
3. **Prepare the feedback safely.** Remove duplicates, preserve source identifiers, separate
   customer words from staff summaries, and avoid exposing unnecessary personal information.
4. **Build a practical theme set.** Start with a small coding scheme for problem, request,
   praise, confusion, workaround, and outcome, then refine themes from the evidence.
5. **Code consistently.** Allow one item to support multiple themes, record coding confidence,
   and keep ambiguous items in a review group rather than forcing a label.
6. **Count with context.** Report item count, share of reviewed feedback, source distribution,
   segment distribution, and trend over time when comparable data exists.
7. **Estimate impact.** Consider severity, frequency, affected segment, blocked outcomes,
   retention or revenue relevance, and operational cost. Label estimates and assumptions.
8. **Ground themes in evidence.** Include representative excerpts or precise summaries with
   source identifiers. Do not invent quotes or treat one vivid example as prevalence.
9. **Find tensions and gaps.** Surface conflicting needs, minority high-impact problems,
   missing segments, and questions the existing feedback cannot answer.
10. **Propose action candidates.** Link each candidate to evidence, expected customer outcome,
    owner, validation need, and tradeoffs. Do not present candidates as approved commitments.

## Output format

- Analysis scope, source coverage, volume, segments, and known bias.
- Ranked themes with counts, percentages, trend, confidence, and impact.
- Representative evidence tied to stable source identifiers.
- Conflicting needs, minority risks, and unanswered questions.
- Prioritized action candidates with rationale, owner, and validation need.
- Limits on interpretation and the next review trigger.
