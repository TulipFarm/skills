# Decision Log

Preserve why a decision was made, not only what was chosen. A useful entry lets a future
reader distinguish a settled decision from a proposal, assumption, or passing comment.

## When to use

Use this skill to capture decisions from meeting notes, messages, documents, or transcripts,
or to consolidate confirmed decisions about one initiative. Do not infer agreement when the
source shows only discussion or silence.

## How to build a decision log

1. **Define the source boundary.** List the materials, dates, and time period covered so
   readers know what evidence was considered.
2. **Find decision signals.** Look for explicit approval, selection, commitment, rejection,
   or direction. Treat suggestions and unresolved debates as candidates, not decisions.
3. **State the decision precisely.** Write one declarative sentence describing what changes,
   for whom, and within what scope.
4. **Capture status and timing.** Record proposed, approved, superseded, or reversed status,
   plus decision date and effective date when available.
5. **Record the accountable owner.** Name the role or person responsible for the decision
   and distinguish that owner from action-item owners.
6. **Summarize options considered.** Include credible alternatives and the selected option.
   Do not create options that were not present in the source material.
7. **Preserve the rationale.** Capture the criteria, evidence, constraints, and tradeoffs
   that drove the choice. Cite the supporting source beside the rationale.
8. **Note consequences and dependencies.** Record affected work, known risks, conditions,
   and follow-up actions without turning the entry into a full project plan.
9. **Define review triggers.** State what date, metric, event, assumption change, or new
   evidence should cause the decision to be revisited.
10. **Resolve ambiguity safely.** Flag conflicting accounts or missing approval and request
    confirmation before labeling an entry as final.

## Output format

- Decision identifier and concise title.
- Status, decision date, effective date, and accountable owner.
- Decision statement and scope.
- Options considered, selected option, and rationale.
- Evidence citations, constraints, consequences, and dependencies.
- Review date or trigger.
- Open verification questions for any item not clearly confirmed.
