# Expert Knowledge Interview

Capture how experienced people actually make judgments, especially the parts they no longer
think to explain. The interviewer must probe for evidence without putting answers in the expert's mouth.

## When to use

Use this skill for handoffs, continuity planning, onboarding, or documenting work that depends
on experience and judgment. It produces verified operational knowledge, not a performance
assessment, interrogation, or substitute for observing real work.

## How to interview an expert

1. **Define the knowledge target.** Name the task, decisions, audience, and situations the
   package must cover. Keep the session narrow enough to reach operational detail.
2. **Prepare from existing material.** Review available notes and documents, then list known
   facts, suspected gaps, and terms that need the expert's definition.
3. **Start with a real case.** Ask the expert to walk through the last representative example
   from trigger to outcome. Concrete recall produces better detail than abstract description.
4. **Probe decision points.** Ask what cues they notice, options they reject, tradeoffs they
   weigh, and what would cause them to choose differently.
5. **Surface exceptions.** Explore difficult cases, common failure modes, warning signs,
   escalation thresholds, and situations where the normal approach should stop.
6. **Capture artifacts and language.** Record the non-sensitive checklists, inputs, outputs,
   definitions, and examples the expert relies on. Do not request credentials or secrets.
7. **Test for teachability.** Restate the process and ask the expert to correct it. Use
   counterexamples to reveal missing conditions or hidden assumptions.
8. **Separate knowledge from opinion.** Label confirmed practice, personal preference,
   organizational requirement, uncertain memory, and proposed improvement.
9. **Draft the package from evidence.** Cite interview segments and supplied artifacts.
   Never fill silence or ambiguity with invented steps.
10. **Run expert verification.** Present decisions, cues, exceptions, and open questions
    for explicit correction and approval before treating the package as authoritative.

## Output format

- Interview scope, expert role, intended audience, and source inventory.
- Operational overview with triggers, inputs, outputs, and boundaries.
- Decision map showing cues, options, rationale, and escalation thresholds.
- Exceptions, failure signals, recovery guidance, and representative examples.
- Glossary of expert terms and assumptions.
- Verification status, corrections received, and unresolved questions.
