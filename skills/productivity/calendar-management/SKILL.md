# Calendar Management

Good scheduling protects focus, travel time, preparation, and decision quality. A free slot
is not automatically a workable slot.

## When to use

Use this skill to assess meeting requests, find workable times, resolve conflicts, or prepare
calendar change proposals. It may recommend additions, moves, or cancellations, but must not
apply them or notify attendees without explicit human confirmation.

## How to manage a calendar

1. **Clarify the scheduling objective.** Identify the meeting purpose, required outcome,
   duration, deadline, location or time-zone constraints, and whether it is actually needed.
2. **Separate required and optional attendees.** Treat decision-makers, presenters, and
   informed participants differently. Do not block scheduling on optional attendance.
3. **Check the full conflict picture.** Review overlaps, adjacent commitments, travel,
   focus blocks, working hours, recurring events, and preparation time.
4. **Respect time zones and boundaries.** Show options in the relevant local times and
   avoid assuming that an open time outside normal hours is acceptable.
5. **Protect preparation and transition time.** Add realistic buffers for reading,
   materials, travel, setup, and recovery between demanding sessions.
6. **Generate ranked options.** Offer a small set of times with tradeoffs. Prefer options
   that satisfy all required attendees and minimize disruption.
7. **Handle conflicts explicitly.** State which event would need to move, who is affected,
   and why the proposed change is preferable. Never silently displace an existing commitment.
8. **Check meeting readiness.** Identify missing agenda, pre-read, decision owner, location,
   access details, or materials that should exist before the event.
9. **Draft the change request.** Prepare concise invitation or rescheduling language without
   sending it. Include purpose, expected preparation, and response deadline.
10. **Require confirmation.** Present the exact proposed additions, edits, cancellations,
    and attendee notifications for human approval before any calendar state changes.

## Output format

- Scheduling objective, constraints, and required attendees.
- Ranked options with local times, conflicts, buffers, and tradeoffs.
- Recommended option and the reason it is safest.
- Preparation checklist and missing meeting details.
- Draft invitation or change note, clearly marked unsent.
- Exact calendar changes awaiting explicit human confirmation.
