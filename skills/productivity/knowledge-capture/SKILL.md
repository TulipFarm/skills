# Knowledge Capture

Turn scattered source material into a small, durable knowledge entry. Preserve provenance
and useful context without attempting to reorganize the entire knowledge base.

## When to use

Use this skill after a discussion, investigation, handoff, or document review when useful
knowledge should be reusable later. Use a broader knowledge-management workflow when the task
is taxonomy design, migration, deduplication, or repository-wide cleanup.

## How to capture knowledge

1. **Define the entry purpose.** State who will use the knowledge, for what task, and the
   narrow question the entry should answer.
2. **Inventory source material.** Assign short source identifiers and record source type,
   date, author or owner, and relevant location.
3. **Extract durable facts.** Keep decisions, definitions, constraints, procedures, lessons,
   and known exceptions. Exclude chatter, repetition, and claims unrelated to the purpose.
4. **Separate evidence from interpretation.** Label direct source facts, reasonable synthesis,
   and unresolved assumptions so future readers can judge confidence.
5. **Write the reusable core.** Lead with a concise answer or principle, then add only the
   context needed to apply it correctly.
6. **Preserve boundaries.** Record where the knowledge applies, where it does not apply,
   and conditions that would make it stale.
7. **Cite at the point of use.** Attach source identifiers to factual statements and quoted
   language. Do not rely on a source list alone.
8. **Identify ownership and maintenance.** Name the responsible role, captured date, and
   a review trigger such as policy change, system change, or elapsed time.
9. **Link related knowledge sparingly.** Include directly relevant entries or source material
   without attempting to redesign folders, tags, or navigation.
10. **Flag gaps for verification.** List contradictions, missing context, and questions for
    a subject expert. Never invent an answer to make the entry appear complete.

## Output format

- Entry title, purpose, audience, owner, and captured date.
- Concise knowledge statement followed by supporting context.
- Scope, constraints, exceptions, and practical implications.
- Claim-level source citations and a compact source inventory.
- Confidence or validity notes with a review trigger.
- Open questions requiring verification.
