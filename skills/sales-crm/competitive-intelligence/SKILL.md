# Competitive Intelligence

Competitive intelligence is not a feature comparison table — it is an
understanding of how competitors win, where they lose, and how to position
against them in a live deal. The output must be actionable for a rep, not just
informative for a strategist.

## When to use

Use this skill before building a sales battlecard, when a specific competitor
comes up in a deal, when preparing for a competitive evaluation, or when updating
quarterly competitive positioning. Do not use it to disparage competitors —
the purpose is to understand the landscape and help reps win fairly.

## How to gather competitive intelligence

1. **Define the competitive scope.** Name the specific competitor and the market
   segment where competition occurs. Not all competitors are equal in every segment
   — be specific about where the overlap is meaningful.

2. **Research their positioning.** Read their homepage, product pages, pricing page,
   and most recent blog posts. Answer: What do they claim to be? What customer
   problem do they say they solve? What is their primary value proposition?

3. **Analyze their product.** Review public demos, documentation, changelog, and
   G2 / Capterra / Trustpilot reviews. What features do they emphasize? What do
   customers praise? What do customers complain about?

4. **Understand their go-to-market.** Target customers (company size, industry,
   buyer persona), pricing model and public pricing signals, sales motion
   (product-led, sales-led, channel), and key integration partners.

5. **Synthesize strengths and weaknesses.** Based on evidence, not assumption:
   - Strengths: what they genuinely do better or are known for.
   - Weaknesses: documented gaps, customer complaints, structural limitations.
   Be honest — overstating competitor weaknesses gives reps false confidence.

6. **Identify win/loss patterns.** Where do deals typically favor this competitor?
   Where do you win against them? Pull from CRM win/loss data if available, or
   from sales team interviews. This section is the most operationally valuable.

7. **Build the counter-positioning.** For each area where the competitor is strong,
   develop a positioning response: how to reframe the comparison, what question
   to ask to shift the evaluation criteria, what proof point counters the claim.

8. **Define the trap-setting questions.** Questions a rep can ask in discovery
   that surface requirements the competitor cannot meet.

## Output format

```
Competitive Brief: <Competitor name>
Segment: <where competition occurs> | Prepared: <date>

## Overview
<Two-to-three sentence summary: who they are, who they target, and why they win>

## Their positioning
- Value prop: <what they claim>
- Target customer: <ICP>
- Pricing model: <how they charge>

## Strengths
- <Genuine strength with evidence>
- ...

## Weaknesses
- <Documented gap or complaint>
- ...

## Where they win vs. us
- <Scenario or deal type where they tend to win and why>

## Where we win vs. them
- <Scenario or deal type where we win and why>

## Counter-positioning

| Their claim | Our response |
|------------|-------------|
| <"We have the most integrations"> | <"We have X — which of your tools is critical?"> |

## Trap-setting questions
- <Question that surfaces a gap they cannot fill>
- ...

## Sources
- <URL or reference — date>
```
