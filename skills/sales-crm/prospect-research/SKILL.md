# Prospect Research

Walk into the call knowing who you are talking to. Assemble a tight, factual brief that
gives a rep the context to be relevant — and stop at research; writing the message is
a separate job.

## When to use

Use this skill when asked to research a prospect, company, or account before outreach,
a discovery call, or a meeting. Reach for it whenever a rep needs background fast and
you want signal over a wall of copied text.

## How to research

1. **Anchor the basics in one pass.** What the company does, rough size, industry,
   region, and the contact's role and tenure. This frames everything else; get it right
   before going deeper.
2. **Separate fact from inference, visibly.** Mark what is confirmed versus what you are
   reasoning ("likely scaling support — three open CS roles"). A rep who can't tell the
   two apart will repeat your guess as fact on the call and lose trust.
3. **Hunt for trigger events.** Funding, leadership changes, a launch, expansion,
   hiring spikes, a public pain point. These are the timely hooks that make outreach
   land; a brief without a trigger is just a profile.
4. **Map likely needs to what you offer.** Translate observations into a hypothesis:
   "growing headcount → onboarding strain → fit for X". State it as a hypothesis to test,
   not a diagnosis. The rep validates it live.
5. **Note the relationship surface.** Prior touches, mutual connections, shared
   community or events, existing accounts at the company. Warm beats cold; surface any
   thread worth pulling.
6. **Keep it to one page and cite where it matters.** If a rep can't skim it before a
   call, it is too long. Trim ruthlessly; load-bearing facts get a source.

## Output format

```
Prospect Brief — <contact>, <company> (<date>)
Snapshot: <what they do> · <size> · <industry/region> · <contact role/tenure>
Triggers: <recent events worth referencing>
Likely needs (hypotheses): <observation → inferred need>
Hooks: <relevant angles / warm threads / mutual ground>
Open questions: <what to confirm on the call>
```

Hand off the brief, not a draft message — turning hooks into copy is the outreach
skill's job.
