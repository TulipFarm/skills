# Call Prep

Good call prep is the difference between a conversation that advances the deal
and one that wastes both sides' time. The brief must be specific enough to be
useful — a generic company summary is not call prep.

## When to use

Use this skill before any substantive sales call: discovery, demo, proposal
review, negotiation, or executive sponsor meeting. Especially important for calls
with new stakeholders, after a deal has stalled, or before a high-stakes
meeting with multiple participants.

## How to prepare a call brief

1. **Account context.** Summarize what matters about this account in three to five
   bullets: industry, size, key business model facts, any recent news (funding,
   layoffs, new product, leadership change) that might affect the deal. Source this
   from the CRM, LinkedIn, press releases, and the company website. Do not pad with
   generic information available to anyone.

2. **Deal history.** What has happened so far? List previous calls, demos, proposals,
   and any commitments made. Note the current stage and what needs to happen to
   advance it. If the deal is stalled, state why it stalled.

3. **Stakeholder map.** For each participant on the call:
   - Name, title, role in the buying decision (champion, economic buyer, blocker,
     influencer, end user).
   - What they care about (their personal win and their business objective).
   - Any known objections or concerns from previous interactions.

4. **Goals for this call.** State one primary desired outcome: the specific
   advancement the rep is trying to achieve (next meeting scheduled, proposal
   accepted, technical approval received). Secondary goals are fine but the primary
   one must be explicit — "have a good conversation" is not a goal.

5. **Proposed agenda.** A three-to-five item agenda the rep can share at the
   start of the call. Each item is a topic and a time allocation. End with next steps
   as the last agenda item — build the ask into the structure.

6. **Anticipated objections and responses.** Based on the stakeholder map and
   deal history, list the two or three most likely objections and a prepared
   response to each. Objection responses should acknowledge the concern, reframe,
   and redirect — not dismiss.

7. **Open questions.** Questions the rep needs answered on this call to advance
   the deal. Prioritize: if only five minutes remain, which one question matters most?

## Output format

```
Call Brief: <Account name>
Call date: <date> | Call type: <discovery / demo / proposal / exec / negotiation>

## Account context
- <Bullet 1: industry, size, business model>
- <Bullet 2: recent news relevant to the deal>
- <Bullet 3: any other relevant context>

## Deal history
- Stage: <current stage>
- Last contact: <date — what was discussed>
- Blockers / reason stalled: <if applicable>

## Stakeholders on this call

| Name | Title | Role | Cares about | Known concerns |
|------|-------|------|------------|----------------|
| ...  | ...   | ...  | ...        | ...            |

## Goal for this call
**Primary:** <specific advancement — e.g., "Schedule technical evaluation with their engineering lead">

## Proposed agenda
1. <Topic> — <X min>
2. ...
5. Next steps — 5 min

## Anticipated objections

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| <e.g., "We already have a solution"> | <Acknowledge + reframe + ask> |

## Must-answer questions
1. <Most important question to get answered>
2. ...
```
